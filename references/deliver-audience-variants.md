# Delivering the QA report — choosing whose document it is

Split verbatim out of `dsc-website-qa` SKILL.md §5 so the delivery rules stay readable. Each paragraph is the original text in full.

**Ship one document addressed to whoever has to act — the working pack is not the handover.** When the work lands with a third party (the build developer, the client, a feed provider), give them a *report*: a rendered house-style PDF/DOCX with a cover, numbered sections, the evidence inline, and the verification commands they can run to check you. Mechanical outputs — a rules file, a CSV, a README, a findings list — are adjuncts to it, never a substitute, and a session that ships only those gets asked "where's the report to give to <name>?" Produce the addressed report in the same pass rather than waiting to be asked, save it beside its source as `<CLIENT>-<topic>-handover-<date>.md`, render it with the house renderer, and say in the reply which file is the one to send and which are reference. Structure it per item with the fix attached, not as a severity-sorted list — the reader has to act, not triage.

**When Woody asks for the findings as "a message to <developer>"** he wants a copy-pasteable handover, not a précis of the report. Save it beside the report as `<CLIENT>-message-to-<name>.md`, and offer the report PDF separately as an attachment. Build it in this order:

1. **A short context paragraph** — what was reviewed, the go-live date, the verdict in a phrase, and what is genuinely good about the build. The praise is not padding: it is what makes the critical section credible to the person who built it.
2. **"Before anything else"** — only the items that make a launch *unsafe* (staging indexability, missing canonicals, false claims, data-integrity bugs), each as wrong → evidence → fix. Put the evidence inline: the offending markup, the curl output, the matching md5.
3. **"Before go-live"** — the dated work: robots.txt and sitemap, the 301 map, share-link hosts, consent and analytics, schema, Open Graph, titles, orphan pages, server config.
4. **"Worth doing in the same pass"** — the Mediums, as one-liners.
5. **"Not on you — needs the client"** — everything the builder cannot decide: trading name, compliance wording, company registration, product-line changes. This section takes items even where they *outrank* the build fixes in severity, because the reader cannot action them; say who is chasing what.
6. **Your own caveats** — the testing you could not do (no rendered-browser pass: console errors, mobile menu, whether forms actually send) and what is still moving on the client's side.

**A report for the client is a different document from the developer handover — and Woody will ask for it separately ("draft me a report to give to <name>").** The developer version is a paste-ready `.md`; the client version is the *rendered* house-style PDF/DOCX addressed to the named contact at the dealership. Same findings, re-cut for a non-technical reader: plain English with no `curl` output, tag names or header names in the body; a short "what's working well" section so it reads as a review rather than a hit list; and **every item owner-labelled** (`your web developer` / `Digital Sales Consultants` / `a decision only you can make`) so the client can forward it without decoding it. Close with a numbered **"what we need from you"** list — confirm the disputed figure, name who applies the fixes — rather than a technical appendix, and keep the compliance findings' professional-review footer. Re-order and re-word by what the reader can act on, but **do not demote a finding's severity to suit the audience**. Where the audiences are an agency/developer *and* the dealer, ship two documents in the same pass rather than one compromise.

**A third variant: the peer note, when the document is going between us rather than to a client.** For a note to the coordinator and whoever hosts the sites, Woody wants it to read as colleagues talking, not a formal report: first names in the `For:` / `From:` lines, first person throughout ("I'm taking the template work", "which I'll carry out"), phases labelled by who does them — "the template patch (mine)", "the feed (the platform owner's)" — instead of by company, no company wordmark or logo mark on the cover, and one line near the top splitting the work. Its purpose is to agree a division of labour, so state plainly which parts are ours and which are the reader's. Every measurement, severity and correction stays identical; only the voice and the furniture change. **When he then names the parties back in, take the instruction as it is given but check what the word also names:** "replace the platform with the platform owner" and, in the next breath, "replace PAUL WOOD with DSC / the platform" are both about *people and letterhead*, while `<platform-host>`, a `.platform` build and the server hostname are the product — ask rather than renaming a platform after a person.

**Before the client version is finalised, get Woody's disposition on every finding — what the client sees is his call, and the answer is not always "all of them".** Three buckets come back, and each removes or re-labels an item: (1) **ours to fix** — anything sitting in a channel DSC manages (the Google Business Profile, a DSC-run ad account, a post on the profile) is actioned by us and must **not** appear in the client document as a client or developer action at all; confirm which channels we hold access to before drafting, and tell the client we have handled those rather than listing them; (2) **the developer's known issue** — something the build agency has already raised or owns, which Woody will not re-report to the client; (3) **a deliberate feature, not a defect** — a live-stock counter in the header, an intentional `noindex`, a count that is meant to be site-wide. Two attribution and tone rules settle with them: **name the third party once the client confirms who builds the site** — use the company name throughout, not `your web developer`, because the client forwards the document straight to them; and **do not put the client's own compliance position in front of them unless Woody says so.** DSC flags FCA issues internally at full severity (see `dsc-fca-compliance`), but a client-facing document must not position DSC as advising on the client's regulatory arrangements — raise those with Woody and let him route them. Withdrawing an item is **not** the same as demoting it, so record every withdrawal in the technical report as a short "client decisions" section naming what was held back and why, and leave the internal severity untouched.

**When the client says "send it to the developer yourself", the deliverable is an EMAIL — not another PDF, and not prose describing an email.** Woody asks for it as a draft he will send ("draft the email to <developer> with <contact> cc'd"). Return it as labelled header lines plus a copy-paste body, then a separate list of anything you could not verify:

```
To: <developer's desk address>
Cc: <client contact>
Subject: <Client> — website review, fixes for <developer>
Attach: <the rendered report PDF>
```

Body, in this order: three sentences of context (what was reviewed, that the client has the report and asked you to pass the findings straight on, that it is attached, that nothing was changed); then the fixes **ordered by what the developer can act on, not by severity**, each a bolded one-liner plus its fix, so they can triage without opening the attachment; one line naming the items that are **ours** ("the meta descriptions and the reviews feed are mine, leave those with me") so they do not ask; one line for anything you could not check; then a request to confirm which items they will pick up and when, with an offer of a call. Sign as Woody with the published DSC contact details.

**Evidence the recipient address, and expect a ticket desk rather than a person.** Search prior correspondence for the domain rather than taking an address from the agency's website or from memory (`himalaya envelope search -a <acct> "from <pattern>"` — mechanics in `references/platform-audit-patterns.md`). A build agency's address is usually its **support desk**, and replies come back tagged `Re:[## <ref> ##]` — that tag is the sign you are on the right route, so address the desk and let the ticket log it. **Do not carry an address forward from an old thread:** individual contacts move between firms and across personal domains, so an address that was right two years ago can belong to somebody else now. When you cannot evidence the address, put a placeholder in the `Cc:` line and say so in the reply — a draft naming the wrong person at the client's supplier is worse than a blank field Woody fills in.
